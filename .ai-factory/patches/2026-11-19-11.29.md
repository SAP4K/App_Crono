# Preserve the finger speed at Android wheel release

**Date:** 2026-11-19 11:29
**Files:** `modules/crono-duration-wheel/android/src/main/java/ro/appcrono/durationwheel/DurationWheelRelease.kt`, `modules/crono-duration-wheel/android/src/main/java/ro/appcrono/durationwheel/CronoDurationWheelModule.kt`, `modules/crono-duration-wheel/android/src/test/java/ro/appcrono/durationwheel/DurationWheelReleaseTest.kt`, `modules/crono-duration-wheel/android/build.gradle`
**Severity:** medium

## Problem

In a scripted slow drag on Redmi Note 9, Timer and Planning moved from about 3 recorded pixels per frame during contact to 5 after release. The glide eased into the selected row after that jump.

## Root Cause

The fitted release raised the measured finger speed to a minimum derived from the remaining distance and a preferred glide duration. A recorded 357 px/s release with 150 px left to the selected row launched at 755 px/s. Using the magnitude also allowed finger motion away from the target to feed the new glide.

## Solution

- Calculate signed content velocity from the Android drag event. Cap the fitted launch at the measured speed, Android's original launch speed and the existing upper speed limit. Round toward zero so the integer velocity honors the cap.
- Use Android's cubic centering when the measured speed is near rest, points away from the target, or cannot fit a bounded coast. Both paths keep the row already chosen by React Native and use the same scroller, touch cancellation and terminal observer. [Android 12 OverScroller source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/android-12.0.0_r1/core/java/android/widget/OverScroller.java#L655)
- Add opt-in `[FIX:wheel-release]` logs under the `CronoWheelRelease` Android DEBUG tag. Log the release inputs, selected motion and failed centering start.

## Verification

The four `:crono-duration-wheel:testReleaseUnitTest` cases failed with the old release calculation and passed with the fix. They cover the recorded slow release in both directions, a brisk flick, near rest, motion away from the target and a glide beyond the time bound. A separate probe ran the compiled release calculation and Android's actual OverScroller on the Redmi. The slow cases launched at ±357 px/s and reached their exact targets. The representative slow native progress took about one second, including visible movement after release.

The installed test APK matched SHA-256 `893079533259a1c4721a878d582caf7034805702e3791010c641df03cf12d8fb`. Repeating the earlier gesture matrix produced 15 measurable glides per screen. The first slow release in each screen stayed near 3 recorded pixels per frame across the handoff, then fell to 2 and 1. Early presses across two moving columns were rejected. On the first tested eligible press, Timer started at `00:17:12` and Planning applied its displayed `02:15:15` to the draft.

## Prevention

- Measure the last part of contact and the first frames after release when testing easing on Android. Compare the last moving frames and the separate stop-to-enabled interval as well.
- Include both signed directions, a nearly stopped release, a short endpoint and a target beyond the available coast. Check exact row selection and that an action stays gated until the last moving column settles.
