# Shorten the Android duration-wheel delay after scrolling stops

**Date:** 2026-11-19 01:35
**Files:** `src/components/DurationWheel.tsx`, `modules/crono-duration-wheel/android/src/main/java/ro/appcrono/durationwheel/CronoDurationWheelModule.kt`
**Severity:** medium

## Problem

The Android wheel already accepted real stops ahead of the standard React Native event. The button still took more than 100 ms to show as enabled in 28 of 32 measured gestures in Timer and Planning.

## Root Cause

The observer checked `OverScroller` during the frame's animation callbacks. `ScrollView` advances the scroller while drawing, so the check could miss a finish reached during that traversal and wait another frame. The baseline diagnostic recorded 48–50 ms from the last native scroll update to confirmed stop and another 28–53 ms from JS receipt to the button's React layout commit. An accepted value recreated every row's press and text elements, even though the selection tint changed on two rows. [Android frame phases](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/android-12.0.0_r1/core/java/android/view/Choreographer.java)

## Solution

- Schedule the Android stop check from an asynchronous main-thread handler after the current animation frame's traversal. Keep the attached-view, current-release and pending-owner checks, the bounded fallback and both scroller completion checks.
- Memoize the native row around its own selected state, geometry, style and stable choice callback. The existing controls still own selection, validation and combined busy state.
- Use `EXPO_PUBLIC_CRONO_WHEEL_LATENCY_LOG=1` at bundle time to record release, confirmation and fallback errors with the `[FIX:wheel-latency]` prefix. Ordinary builds keep the logging disabled. The diagnostic APKs were separate from the retained clean test release.

## Verification

The same scripted gesture and frame-analysis workflow measured 32 moving samples per revision on the Android 12 Redmi Note 9. The Timer baseline uses the retained clean capture of the previously installed build. Planning used a fresh baseline capture of that build. Stop-to-enabled time is estimated from the recorded frames.

| Caller | Earlier median / max (ms) | Updated median / max (ms) | Updated at most 100 ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Timer | 118.8 / 140.0 | 98.9 / 120.3 | 11/17 |
| Planning | 123.4 / 167.4 | 114.9 / 121.0 | 5/15 |

The corrected instrumented comparison observed 34–35 ms from the last native scroll update to confirmed stop and 20–33 ms from JS receipt to layout commit across five samples. Both actions rejected a press during a genuine coast. Timer started the displayed 02:04:28 and Planning applied the displayed 02:02:15 to its draft. Lint, typecheck, 322 Node tests, 24 focused browser scenarios, Android release assembly and Android/iOS/web exports passed.

The signed test package `ro.appcrono.wheelstoptest` was verified against APK SHA256 `6c03f7cb8a9d964be6267db28a92262f118eb2e738b44b3ef80c300be7f44ee2`. The Hermes source map and staged Kotlin inputs matched the code. Logs and recordings are in ignored `.expo/duration-wheel-stop/latency-fix/`. The 100 ms target was attained in 16 of 32 measurable samples. Physical measurements cover Android.

## Prevention

- Check delivery, React commit and the enabled frame separately. Keep the gesture and frame-analysis settings identical when comparing releases.
- Preserve native ownership and actual scroller completion. A spline can briefly round to the final pixel while its animation remains active. [Android OverScroller implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/android-12.0.0_r1/core/java/android/widget/OverScroller.java)
- Keep the stable row props and verify row choice, gesture takeover, selection tint and both action guards when changing the native picker.
